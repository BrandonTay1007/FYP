## 1. Mark weights (rubric v260915, out of 100)

| Criterion | Marks | Team or individual |
|---|---|---|
| Pitch content | 30 | Team |
| Content and contribution | 20 | Individual |
| Presentation fluency | 20 | Individual |

| Visual communication | 5 | Team |

Each criterion is marked out of 10 and scaled. General standard: "Meeting all requirements well, or simply having no obvious weaknesses, is not by itself sufficient for an HD." Completing the list gets P/C. D/HD needs something beyond the list (mark release post, 10 Oct).

## 2. What "Pitch content" (30 marks) actually scores

The HD band is specific, and most of it is the new material, not the intro:

- Project goals, users, use cases and requirements, with "the relevance and importance of significant requirements to end users" explained.
- How key features address those requirements "through an effective wireframe walk-through". This is the single most explicit HD phrase in the rubric.
- A feasible approach "supported by appropriate testing, project management methodology, WBS, schedule, dependencies and milestones". Testing is named here even though the Ed content list never mentions it.
- Scope, anticipated challenges and feasibility "critically and realistically considered".
- A conclusion with "meaningful, evidence-based reflection on teamwork and project management".
- Everything connected "with no material gaps".

The D band says the same things with "only minor gaps in depth, justification, detail or connection". The gap between D and HD is linkage and justification, not more slides.

## 3. Effort priorities, in order

Quoted phrases are verbatim from the rubric. The ordering, the "D versus HD gap" framing and the tactics are agent inference from comparing the bands, not staff guidance. Staff never ranked the content items on Ed.

1. **Requirements to wireframe linkage.** Walk through the wireframes and, for each screen, name the requirement it satisfies and the user need behind it. This is the biggest gap between a D and an HD in the band wording.
2. **Plan with dependencies and milestones, plus a testing approach.** The rubric wants dependencies and milestones called out, not just a Gantt bar chart. Add a short testing slide even though Ed did not ask for it.
3. **Three project-specific risks**, each with impact and a mitigation, introduced as the top 3 of a larger set (staff, 11 Oct). Generic lists read as Pass level. The rest of the register is for questions, not the slide.
4. **Conclusion with evidence.** Cite actual things from meeting minutes or sprint work, not platitudes. The rubric says "evidence-based" twice.
5. **Individual content.** 20 marks per person hinge on each presenter showing "deep, project-specific understanding" of their own section. The split from Meeting 4 (plan and scope to Brandon and Joshua, wireframes to Yu Hong and Zhi Hong, risks to Victor) should map to who can answer questions on it. Q&A (5 marks) targets at least one question per member.

## 4. Intro depth

Keep it to about one minute. The panel heard the mid-sem pitch, and staff said the final deck is the corrected mid-sem slides plus new content. No rubric criterion rewards re-explaining background. "Structure and flow" (15 marks) rewards a "purposeful opening" and "logical progression".

Intro should do three things, then hand to the problem statement:
- Title slide: project, team PCS-33, member names (presenter name on every slide they speak to).
- One or two sentences on what ParaShot is and who it is for.
- One line on what changed since mid-sem and what is new today.

Do not re-run the WSM or option analysis (staff, 5 Oct: WSM is done). Changes since mid-sem may be highlighted briefly to save time (staff, 10 Oct).

Staff, 12 Oct: pointing back to the mid-sem pitch is optional, and the panel may not remember it. A one-line acknowledgement is enough. Anything this rubric scores (requirements, wireframes, plan, risks) has to be said again, briefly, on its own slide.

Ed has no staff definition of "intro material" beyond "1. Introduction slide". The only related thread (11 Sep) is a student reply saying to name every member.

## 5. Mid-sem lessons that cost marks (PCS-33 feedback)

Team criteria all scored D. Individual criteria scored HD except professional conduct (D).

Fix list from the feedback, all cheap and worth roughly 50 marks of rubric weight across structure, visuals and conduct:
- Slide text too small and too wordy. Fewer words, larger type.
- Went over time by a bit. Time each speaker individually. 14 minutes total for 5 members, 5% per minute over, stopped at 1 minute over.
- Non-presenters moved around and distracted. Stand still, face the audience.
- Dress. Treat it as a formal graded assessment. No slippers or shorts.
- Muddled hand-off to the wrong person. Rehearse transitions until automatic.
- Individual notes: watch volume (borderline shouting), look at the screen less, keep eye contact inclusive.
- Cohort-wide penalties (10 Oct): holding a phone, reading from notes or script.
