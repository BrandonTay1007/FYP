## Front matter (0 marks, not counted)

### Title page
- Report title, team number, unit codes.
- Team member names.
- **Total word count**, stated clearly. *(W 1d)*

### Authorship statement
- **Missing = automatic 50% penalty.** It must sit in the front cover material. *(W IMPORTANT)*
- (i) Against each member's initials: the sections they **led**, meaning they drafted the first version **and** edited and finalised others' contributions. *(W 1c i)*
- (ii) The figures, diagrams, tables or other major elements each member produced or led. *(W 1c ii)*
- Commenting and small edits are assumed equal and need not be listed. *(W 1c)*
- Layout is flexible ("both methods are fine"). *(Ed 3641037)*

### Table of contents
- Numbered headings that match the body. List of figures and list of tables recommended. *(R style)*

### Optional
- Acknowledgements or other items, at the team's discretion. *(W 1e)*
- **If any member is on special consideration:** a list at the start of the report giving each late student, their placeholder colour and the page numbers of their placeholders. *(W late submission)*

## 2 Background material / literature review (15 marks, ~650 words)

The worksheet requires three subsections: introduction, body, conclusion. *(W 3a)*

### 2.1 Introduction to the review
- What the review covers and why, so the reader knows the themes ahead.

### 2.2 Review body
Split into themed subsections. What has to appear across them:
- **Previous works:** identify, review and **critique** 5–8 references per student. Staff: 15 minimum for a 3-person team, more for larger teams, and every reference anywhere in the report counts. *(W 3b; Ed 3673458, 3676563)*
- **Software tools** relevant to the topic, "any you can find". *(W 3b)*
- **Existing "competing" tools** that address the main project goals. *(R)*
- **Synthesis:** common themes, ideas, techniques, outcomes, strengths and weaknesses, recent advances and their implications. *(W 3c)* Write it critically, not just descriptively; the C band is "mostly descriptive rather than critical". *(R)*
- **Literature types:** academic **and** grey literature, plus software and hardware where relevant. *(R)*
- **Project components:** points that address the project's proposed parts, e.g. PM processes and techniques, topic, likely use cases, UI design, data analysis technique, user testing. *(R)*
- **Supporting decisions:** material on the project life cycle (predictive, agile etc.), technical choices (software, tools) and the topic area, so each later decision has support. *(W notes 5)*
- **WSM (optional):** a WSM giving the rationale for choices "can go here if needed". *(Ed 3622004)* Staff also call the rubric's "critical analysis of PM methodologies" a WSM for choosing the methodology. *(Ed 3679373)* The later ruling is "follow the worksheet" *(Ed 3685039)*, so the methodology rationale itself belongs in 3.2.1. Use the review only as supporting background.
- Cite in-text in APA.

### 2.3 Conclusion of the review
- Summarise what the literature shows.
- **Justify the project:** state the need, gap or shortcoming in the literature or existing software that the project fills. *(W 3d; R "evidence of a gap/opportunity")*
- Show how the project differs from the competing tools.

**HD looks like:** 15+ highly relevant, diverse sources (academic and grey, plus tools); deep critical analysis of methodologies, gaps and competing tools, clearly linked to the project aims; flawless APA; a logically structured review showing original insight and synthesis. *(R)*

## 3 Project management plan (50 marks, ~1650 words)

Rubric items: scope statement; requirements discussion and RTM; team organisation and roles; PM process; schedule; resources. *(R)* Staff: requirements, scope and limitations are the **finalised versions as of end of semester**. *(Ed 3622004)*

### 3.1 Project scope and requirements (5 marks)

#### 3.1.1 Product characteristics
- What the product is and does at a high level. *(W 4a i)*

#### 3.1.2 Scope statement
- **In-scope** items. *(R)*
- **Out-of-scope** items. *(R)*
- **Priority** for each item (high / low, or must / should / could). *(R)*
- Limitations as at end of semester. *(Ed 3622004)*

#### 3.1.3 Requirements elicitation
- **How the requirements were obtained** (sources and methods). *(R)*

#### 3.1.4 Functional and non-functional requirements
- FR and NFR lists, **as refined since the pitch**. *(W 4a i)*
- SRS-style content (FR and NFR listed) is expected in this unit's proposal. *(Ed 3569271)*
- HD wants an "extensive list" with "detailed explanation". *(R)*
- Say what changed since the pitch.

#### 3.1.5 Acceptance criteria
- "Detailed product acceptance criteria." *(R HD)*
- No fixed format. They describe what the stakeholder or client would accept as satisfactory. *(Ed 3679990)*
- Placement (in the RTM or a separate table) is not ruled on.

#### Pointer to the RTM
- Refer to the full RTM **from the time of the pitch** in Appendix A. *(W 4a ii)* HD wants an "extensive" RTM. *(R)*

### 3.2 Project organisation (15 marks)

#### 3.2.1 Development life cycle model
- The SDLC model: predictive, adaptive, incremental, agile, or a **hybrid** (allowed). *(W 4b i; Ed 3678154)*
- **Rationale** for choosing it. *(W 4b i)*
- **Adaptations** to the team's context. *(W 4b i)*
- HD: "detailed explanation and justification of PM methodology" **and tools used**. *(R)*

#### 3.2.2 Team organisation, roles and responsibilities
- Each member's role and responsibilities, described and discussed. *(R)*

### 3.3 Schedule and resource requirements (10 marks)

#### 3.3.1 Activities and schedule overview
- Overview of the activities and tasks needed "to satisfy the project agreement". *(W 4c i)*
- Major phases, task dependencies and milestones. *(R)*
- Point to the WBS (Appendix C) and Gantt (Appendix D).

#### 3.3.2 Resource requirements
- Estimates of the **total** resources needed. *(W 4c iii)*
- Examples: number and roles of people, computer time, software, hardware. *(W 4c iii)*
- HD: a "detailed list of technical and human resources", showing how they are used across the project. *(R)*

### 3.4 Management process (20 marks)

#### 3.4.1 Risk management
- The **process** used to identify, analyse and manage risks. *(W 4d i)*
- Point to the Risk Register in Appendix B. *(W 4d i)*
- HD: "comprehensive discussion of risk management", with an "extensive Risk Register covering PM **and** technical risk relating to project execution". *(R)*
- Which risks to include depends on the topic; detail the most important. *(Ed 3679992)*

#### 3.4.2 Communication plan
- Communication within the team **and** with other stakeholders. HD: "comprehensive". *(R)*

#### 3.4.3 Monitoring and control
- How progress is monitored against planned milestones. *(W 4d ii)*
- The tools used, e.g. a Kanban board. *(W 4d ii)*

#### 3.4.4 Review and audit
- Review and audit mechanisms, e.g. records of meetings and other documentation, version control. *(W 4d ii)*

**HD looks like (whole PMP):** "outstanding, comprehensive and realistic". Detailed scope with priorities; extensive requirements with elicitation and acceptance criteria; a justified methodology plus tools; detailed roles; comprehensive risk management and communication plan; a detailed Gantt; a detailed resource list. *(R)*

## 4 Methodology (10 marks, ~600 words)

Goal: enough detail that "another developer or team" could follow the same steps and get the same results. *(R)* Staff: the proposed solution and software designs go here. *(Ed 3622004)*

### 4.1 Proposed solution overview
- What will be built, in one place, before the detail. *(Ed 3622004)*

### 4.2 Algorithms and machine learning approach
- Any specific algorithms, statistical analyses or ML approaches the software will use. *(W 5a)*
- An overview of each. *(W 5a)*
- High-level pseudocode if helpful. *(W 5a)*
- Justification for each decision. *(R)*

### 4.3 System architecture and integration
- An appropriate diagram (e.g. an architecture diagram) **plus text** explaining how the components integrate. *(W 5b)*

### 4.4 Software design
- Software designs. *(Ed 3622004)*

### 4.5 Data collection and pre-processing
- The data source(s) and how the data will be pre-processed. *(W 5c)* Required for Data Science; for Computer Science only if relevant. Relevant for an ML project.
- Data collection, management and processing steps. *(R)*

### 4.6 Tools and development steps
- **All** required software, tools and utilities, including libraries, programming languages and data management needs. *(R HD)*
- The software development steps, in a clear, logical, step-by-step order. *(R)*

Sample code is **not required** in the updated rubric. Code already written may be kept. *(Ed 3673486)*

**HD looks like:** complete and unambiguous tool list; detailed step-by-step method covering every stage; thorough justification; "fully replicable". *(R)*

- A 1–2 paragraph summary of the report's key contents. *(W 6a)*

## Style and presentation (5 marks)

HD needs: *(R; W style)*
